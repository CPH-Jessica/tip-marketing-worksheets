# AI-Tells Starter Banned List

These are the phrases and habits that make prose read AI-written — especially in heat scenes,
where the stakes for sounding human are highest. Treat Tier 1 as hard zeros. Add your own pet
peeves in the heat-map template; Ember honors both lists.

## TIER 1 — never appear, narration or dialogue

### AI-ism phrases
- electricity coursing through
- a smile tugging at
- couldn't help but
- found myself / found herself
- before I could stop myself
- let out a breath I didn't know I was holding (and every "let out a…" construction)
- something shifted
- I didn't trust my voice
- swallowed hard
- a beat of silence / broke the silence
- searched his/her face
- met/held his/her gaze
- the air between us / them
- my breath catches / breath hitched
- thick air / the air was thick / air crackles
- the words hit / the words land
- the distance between us
- scent of / smell of / smells like (render smell through reaction instead)
- a mix between / a mixture of / something like

### Crutch physical actions
- arms crossed / folded arms
- rolled his/her eyes
- heart hammered / heart pounded
- smirked / smirk
- tangled (sheets or otherwise)
- hitched (breath)

### Heat-scene specific
- slick folds
- feminine core / molten core
- dance of passion / bodies entwined
- "member," "manhood," and every other Latinate euphemism at heat 4–5

### Dialogue tags
Use "said" / "asked," or replace the tag with an action beat. Never:
- growled
- whispered
- softly / slightly as tag adverbs

## TIER 2 — narrated time (reads as AI stage direction)

The narrator never stamps the page with a clock, a date, or a counted span:
- clock times in narration ("at 3:47," "it was 8:15 in the morning")
- calendar dates in narration ("on October 14th")
- time-stamped scene openings ("three days later," "two weeks earlier," "the next morning at 7 a.m.")
- counted durations ("they'd been there forty-three minutes," "it had been six years, four months…")

Ground time through scene instead: light, weather, season, the state of a room (coffee already
on, the bar nearly empty, the first hard frost). **Dialogue exception:** a character may SAY a
time the way people talk ("I told Mama we'd be there by six"); the narrator never stamps it.

## Why this matters double in heat scenes

Under pressure, AI prose reaches for its most-worn phrases — and nothing pressures a model like
an intimate scene. Every cliché above breaks the reader's trance exactly when the trance
matters most. Specifics are the antidote: this character's nervous laugh, this kitchen's
granite, this man's cinnamon gum. If a sentence could appear in anyone's book, it doesn't
belong in yours.
