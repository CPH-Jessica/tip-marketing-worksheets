# Scene Craft — Structure, Register, Checklists

Read before drafting. This file is the how: the skeleton every intimate scene hangs on, the
threshold technique, vocabulary registers per heat level, and the checklists that keep a scene
human instead of choreographed.

## The scene skeleton

1. **Hunger established.** The scene opens already charged — we enter with the want in the
   room, shown through the POV character's body and attention before anyone moves. Start in
   the action (CPH rule); no throat-clearing.
2. **The threshold.** The moment it slips — the beat where tension becomes touch. This is the
   most-botched beat in the genre. Technique below.
3. **Escalation with interiority.** Physical progress and internal experience braided —
   never three consecutive beats of pure choreography. Each escalation lands with what it
   *means* to the POV character.
4. **The peak.** At the pen name's level, fully. This is where the emotional destination
   (named at intake) actually detonates — the surrender, the flip, the claim, the reveal.
5. **Aftermath.** Bodies and feelings after — sensitivity, shaking, awkward laugh, tears that
   surprise her, his uncharacteristic quiet. What changed between them is visible here. Never
   cut for length.

## The threshold technique

Crossing from tension into touch without the gear-grind:

- The threshold is a **decision made visible** — hers, or her unmistakable yes to his. Put it
  in a concrete micro-action: she doesn't step back; she sets down the glass; she reaches first.
- Cross on a held breath, not a scene break. The classic failure is white space where the
  crossing should be — that's a fade, and above heat 2 a fade is a broken promise.
- Slow the prose AT the threshold, not after. Time dilates in the two seconds before the first
  touch; spend sentences there. Once contact lands, rhythm accelerates.
- Let one of them say something true and unpolished right at the line. The genre's best
  thresholds are half dialogue: an admission, a dare, a "we shouldn't" answered with motion.

## Vocabulary register by heat level

| Level | Bodies | Acts | Guiding rule |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1–2 | Hands, mouths, pulse, warmth — nothing below the collarbone gets specific | None on page | Sensation as emotion: what the almost does to her chest, not her body's mechanics |
| 3 | Frank but graceful — skin, thighs, the shape of want | Rendered through sensation, low anatomical specificity | The reader sees through the door; the camera stays on her face more than the mechanics |
| 4 | Anatomy named directly, plain English | Fully rendered, beat by beat | Blunt where heat calls for it, lyrical only where SHE would be lyrical |
| 5 | Blunt, unflinching, the words the characters would actually think | Nothing softened; kink where the brand carries it | No euphemism, no Latin, no coyness — and interiority still runs the whole way through |

Two failure modes, same cost: purple ("her feminine core throbbed") and clinical (anatomical
narration with no one home). The corrective for both is POV — every explicit word filtered
through what this specific character notices, wants, and can't quite believe.

## Build-up checklist (before anyone touches)

- [ ] Involuntary response shown before contact — clench, heat pooling, caught breath (brand-appropriate at 1–2: flush, held breath, awareness)
- [ ] Emotional stakes on the table — what's risked by wanting this
- [ ] Consent current visible — enthusiasm, a check-in, a dare, the reader-visible yes (dark romance: see the reader contract)
- [ ] Voice intact — internal observations, humor, self-doubt in the pen name's cadence

## During checklist

- [ ] Direct language at level — no euphemism above 3, no gratuitous crudity below the brand
- [ ] Vulnerability or internal conflict present mid-act
- [ ] Bodies real and slightly messy — an angle adjusted, a laugh, sensitivity, sweat
- [ ] The scene's emotional destination detonates — trust deepens, control cracks, the flip lands
- [ ] Humor/affection allowed to surface where the pen name carries it
- [ ] Pacing breathes — moments held, not sprinted; no wall-to-wall mechanics

## Aftermath checklist

- [ ] Physical aftermath shown — sensitivity, trembling, tangled limbs are banned but tangled feelings aren't
- [ ] Emotional aftermath shown — tender, awkward, giggly, shattering, or scared of what it meant
- [ ] What changed is legible — the relationship is not where it was a scene ago
- [ ] Voice still present — she is still herself, only more so
- [ ] Dark romance / kink scenes: aftercare beat rendered, the darkness paying its tenderness tax

## Red flags — slash on sight

- Repeated action beats with no new emotion (the second identical kiss teaches nothing)
- Stage-direction choreography — limbs moving with nobody inside them
- A scene any couple could star in unchanged (the swap test — fails = rewrite)
- Voice vanishing at the threshold — the humor and interiority dying the moment clothes come off
- Perfect performance — zero fumbling, zero adjustment, zero humanity
- The unearned scene — heat with no story consequence (flag it to the author; don't pad it)
- A fade above the pen name's floor, or explicitness above its ceiling, without the author's say-so

## Intimacy-specific banned items

The full starter banned list (AI-tell phrases, crutch actions, narrated-time rules) lives in
`ai-tells-starter-list.md` and applies in full, along with anything in the author's own banned
list. The ones that most tempt heat scenes:

- **tangled** (sheets or otherwise), **hitched** (breath)
- **slick folds** — and push past bed/shower default imagery generally
- Dialogue tags **growled, whispered, softly** — use "said" or an action beat
- **the air between us / them**, **air crackles**, **electricity coursing through**
- **swallowed hard**, **let out a breath…**, **my breath catches**
- **scent of / smell of** constructions — render smell through reaction instead
- Narrated clock/duration stamps ("after twenty minutes," "the next morning at 7") — ground
  time in light, sound, and the state of the room
