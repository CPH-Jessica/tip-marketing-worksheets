What readers are actually searching for — and how to line your book up with them
Week 1 Worksheet

Before you write a single word, know what your reader is already hunting for. Readers don't search for "a beautiful story about healing." They search for grumpy sunshine small town romance. Those specific words are called tropes, and the combination you choose is your trope stack.

This worksheet walks you through building a stack that speaks your genre's language, then turns that stack into the exact search phrases, hashtags, and positioning lines that put your book in front of the right readers. Step 3

Niche It Down

A trope stack is only powerful when it's specific enough to feel made for me but familiar enough that readers already want it. Use the guidance for wherever you are:

🌱 New author — start here Pick ONE anchor trope with a Growth or Evergreen signal (that's your visibility) and add ONE flavour trope you love writing (that's your voice). Two clear tropes beats five muddy ones. Example: Grumpy/Sunshine (anchor) + Small Town (flavour). Write that. Sell that. You can widen later.
⚡ Seasoned author — sharpen the edge Keep your proven anchor tropes, then stack a third trope to escape the crowd. Everyone is writing enemies-to-lovers; far fewer are writing enemies-to-lovers + forced proximity + single dad. Cross-check demand with the Trope Compatibility Checker (linked below), and lead your ads with the rarest trope in your stack — that's the reader who can't find this book anywhere else.
