The Scene Justification Test

Every scene must answer YES to at least 2 of these 4 questions. One is not enough. Zero is an automatic cut.

1. Advances Plot? Does something happen that moves the story forward?
2. Deepens Character? Do we learn something meaningful about who this person is?
3. Escalates Tension/Stakes? Does the pressure increase? Does the risk grow?
4. Delivers Genre Promise? Does this scene give the reader what they bought this book for?

2+ YES = Stays  |  1 YES = On Trial  |  0 YES = Cut

Before & After Examples

Example 1 — The Getting-Ready Scene

BEFORE (bloat):
Three pages: Heroine tries on outfits. Calls best friend. They discuss what to wear. She does her makeup. She looks in the mirror and thinks about how nervous she is.

Justification: Maybe 1/4 (deepens character?). On trial.
AFTER (consolidated):
She was already at the restaurant, adjusting a dress she was not sure about, when he walked in.

Same beat. Two sentences. Zero bloat.

The getting-ready montage is one of the most common scenes that fails the justification test. If it does not advance plot or escalate tension, consolidate it into a detail.

Example 2 — The Recap Conversation

BEFORE (redundant):
Heroine tells her best friend everything that happened in the previous chapter. Two pages of dialogue the reader already witnessed.

Justification: 0/4. Reader already knows this.
AFTER (purposeful):
"I told Mara everything." She paused. "Well. Almost everything."

One line. Introduces mystery (what did she leave out?) which escalates tension.

Recap conversations are almost always cut material. If the reader already saw it happen, they do not need to hear it retold.

Example 3 — Load-Bearing Scene

[LOAD-BEARING?] Chapter 6: Coffee shop conversation with sister
Appears to be filler. Sister mentions an ex-boyfriend in passing. No plot advancement, minimal tension. Justification: 1/4 (character).

Recommendation: Flag for author review. The sister's ex-boyfriend mention may set up a reveal in Chapter 18. Do NOT cut without checking the outline.

This is why the load-bearing check matters. What looks like filler might be a planted seed.
