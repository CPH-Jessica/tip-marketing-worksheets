Why eight passes and not one: Ask AI to "edit this chapter" and it does a little of everything and none of it well. Ask it to do one job — just the timeline, just the dialogue, just the heat — and it gets sharp. The system is the sequence. Each pass has its own prompt, its own checklist, and its own definition of done.
Part 1 — The editing system

The eight passes

1

Quality Check

The eleven-point sweep: natural prose, tone match, outline adherence, arc consistency, emotional progression, pacing, show-vs-tell, chapter ending, POV, heat level, and the banned list. Fix, don't just flag.

2

Plot Holes

Find the cracks before you paint the walls. What was set up and never paid off, what contradicts chapter 4, what the reader will notice and you won't.

3

Timeline

Because nothing pulls a reader out faster than a Tuesday that happens twice. Days, seasons, travel time, how long that pregnancy has been going on.

4

Line Edit

Sentence by sentence. Rhythm, repetition, filter words, the adverbs doing work a verb should do.

5

Dialogue Polish

Does each character sound like themselves with the tags stripped off? Subtext, interruption, and the speeches nobody would actually say out loud.

6

Heat Scene Edit

Intimacy edited on its own terms — calibration to your pen name's heat level, emotional beats under the physical ones, and the clichés that kill a scene dead.

7

Developmental Edit — the Slash Editor

The structural pass. What's not earning its place, what's out of order, what the book is actually about versus what you meant it to be about.

8

Voice Audit

The final polish. Does chapter 20 sound like chapter 1? Drift is invisible while you're writing and obvious to a reader.

9

The Human Pass — the one that cannot be outsourced

Everything above gets you a clean book. This is the pass that makes it yours. What only the author can judge, why no tool will ever do it for you, and how to do it properly instead of skipping it because you're tired.

Plus the Progress Tracker — all eight passes across every chapter in one place, so you never lose track of what's done.

The standard we hold the whole class

The rule that runs through all nine passes

"One pass, one job — and if it fails, fix it, don't just flag it."

A pass that hands you a list of problems has done half the work. Every prompt in this class ends the same way: if a check fails, revise it, then tell me what you changed and why. You stay the editor. The tool does the labor.
