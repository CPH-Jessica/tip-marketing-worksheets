Real questions from real authors — the frustrations they brought us, and exactly how the Story Clinic answers each one.
When authors applied to test the Story Clinic, we asked one question: what's your biggest editing struggle right now? The answers were honest, funny, and painfully familiar — AI editors that claim they fixed things they didn't, author voice getting sanded off, backlogs of finished books, and at least one writer who was simply, completely over it. The questions below are drawn from what they told us — lightly paraphrased and anonymized — and every answer is how the Story Clinic actually responds.
What is this, exactly
So what kind of editing is this, exactly — just grammar and spelling?

Grammar and spelling are the smallest things it checks. The Story Clinic runs your book through every layer of editing a publishing house would:

  • Developmental: plot logic, stakes, structure, chapter hooks
  • Continuity & timeline: a story clock that catches "it was Tuesday twice this week"
  • Dialogue: voice separation, said-bookisms, on-the-nose talk
  • Line editing: echoes, filter words, flat prose, pacing at the sentence level
  • Voice & style: keeping the book sounding like you (more on that below)
  • AI-tell removal: a dedicated De-AI sweep, plus a flash-forward pass that kills the most common machine-narration habit

Plus story lab reports — emotional arc, character presence, pacing curve, conflict intensity, timeline — with charts, and a final Clinic Verdict that grades the manuscript and tells you the one thing to fix first.

I already use the multi-pass editing skill from class. Is this for people newer than me?

It's for you especially. The Story Clinic is that editing system — the multi-pass method from our Edit Like a Boss class, grown up. The pass sequence you already know is in here, expanded and updated (including the newest passes that didn't exist when you learned it), and wrapped in things a skill alone can't do: instant offline scans, charts that update as you revise, a chapter list that tracks your progress, and a downloadable Manuscript Report at the end.

If you've run the passes by hand, you'll be dangerous with this thing in about four minutes.

The AI trust problem
My AI keeps telling me things are corrected when they are NOT corrected.
The #1 complaint we heard

The Story Clinic's answer is simple: receipts, not promises.

Every EDIT prompt requires the AI to return a change log — every meaningful change on one line, before → after, tagged with the rule that drove it, with repetitive fixes rolled up and counted ("removed 37 filter words"). The log is the receipt. And anything the AI says it couldn't change without a story decision goes in a separate NEEDS AUTHOR list instead of quietly being skipped.

Then the tool checks the AI's homework: paste the revised chapter back in, and the built-in scanner — which runs on your computer, deterministically, with no AI involved — recounts every flag. You see "flags: 14 → 2" on screen. If the AI claimed a fix it didn't make, the counter says so. The scanner cannot be sweet-talked.

Even the newest, smartest AI models do a terrible job editing — and it was already my least favourite part of the process.

AI editing usually fails for a reason nobody talks about: "edit this" is a terrible prompt. Given a vague instruction, even a top model improvises — it rewrites what it likes, skips what it doesn't, and drifts further off-course the longer the chapter gets.

The Story Clinic never asks vaguely. Each pass hands your AI one job, with a numbered checklist, hard guardrails (preserve plot, POV, and voice exactly; never invent new story content), a required output format, and a size limit so long chapters arrive in complete parts instead of getting cut off mid-sentence. The AI stops being a moody collaborator and starts being a technician following a work order.

And if you don't trust AI edits at all yet? Every pass also has a review-only mode — notes and specific fixes, nothing touched.

I spend a lot of my editing time cursing at my AI.

We can't make your AI stop being your AI. 😄 But nearly all of the cursing, when we dug into it, traces to three things: vague instructions, invisible results, and re-explaining your standards every single session. The Clinic removes all three — the prompt is pre-built and specific, the flag counter shows exactly what changed, and your voice and genre ride along in every prompt (heat level wherever it applies), so you never have to say it twice.

Residual cursing may occur. It'll just be quieter.

Your voice stays yours
I'm losing my author voice. The suggestions lean on a "literary fiction" standard instead of commercial fiction — and the prose that comes back doesn't sound like me.

This one shaped the tool more than any other. Two answers:

1. It's built for commercial genre fiction, full stop. The benchmarks are commercial — dialogue ratios, pacing curves, and emotional-arc patterns drawn from what actually sells in genre fiction, not from a lit-fic ideal of "restraint." There's a dedicated romance edition and a genre edition (fantasy, sci-fi, mystery, horror, thriller) because those shelves have different rules, and the tool respects that.

2. Your voice is a required input, not an afterthought. You set a voice profile once — pick a style lane or paste your own voice notes (there's a helper prompt that distills your voice skill or style notes into an editor-ready voice block). From then on, every single editing prompt carries your voice, and every EDIT prompt adds explicit orders: stay inside this voice the whole way, do not smooth it into generic prose, and — quoting the actual rule — "an edit that loses the voice is worse than the flaw it fixed." Anything that would need a new story decision gets routed to the NEEDS AUTHOR list for you to decide, instead of being invented for you.

Workflow, speed & sanity
I've built up so many pipelines, skills, and plugins that they're all doing pieces of each other's jobs.

The Story Clinic is one page. Not a plugin, not a stack, not seven skills with overlapping opinions — a single file that opens in your browser and contains the entire editing system: the scanner, all the passes in the right order, the story reports, and the final report generator.

It doesn't need to be installed into anything, doesn't conflict with anything, and works with whatever AI you already use — Claude, ChatGPT, Gemini, whichever. The tool builds each prompt; you paste it wherever you like. Your existing setup can stay or go; the Clinic doesn't care.

The inconsistency of these models is killing me — I keep reworking my process every time they change. My editing pace has cratered, I have a backlog of finished books waiting, and even with a technical background I haven't found a better way.

Then you'll recognize what this actually is: your editing SOP, frozen as software.

  • The pass order, rubrics, and rules live in the tool — when a model updates and its behavior shifts, your process doesn't move, because the process was never in the model. Same prompt, same rubric, same order, every session.
  • The built-in scans are deterministic — pure code, no AI. Same chapter in, same flags out, today and in six months. That's your stable baseline no model can wobble.
  • Paste-back re-scanning is, frankly, regression testing for prose: revise, re-run, watch the flag count drop — and catch it immediately if a "fix" introduced new problems.

For the backlog: the Clinic runs chapter by chapter with visible progress per chapter, so a stack of waiting books stops being one unclimbable wall and becomes a checklist that shortens every day.

Honestly? I'm kind of over the whole thing. lol

Fair. Here's the whole workflow, then: paste a chapter, hit Go, read what the scan found, copy the pass prompt into your AI, paste the revision back, watch the flags drop. Repeat. You don't have to be un-over-it — the tool always knows which step is next, so you only ever have to do one small thing at a time.

I have professional editing experience, so I edit my own books from page one — but it's become so time-consuming that I set my latest manuscript aside and started writing the next book instead.

You're exactly who the Clinic serves best, because you already know what good looks like — the problem is the hours, not the judgment.

Let the tool do the grunt sweep an editor's eye shouldn't be spent on: every filter word, echo, crutch phrase, and AI-ism flagged and counted in seconds, plus a first-pass story clock. Let the passes produce the structured findings — ranked concerns with quoted evidence, the way a good editorial memo reads. Then you do what only a trained editor can: make the calls. Every pass hands you a NEEDS AUTHOR list of the calls it refused to make for you — paste it into the pass's notes box and it's saved into your Manuscript Report.

That set-aside manuscript becomes a chapter-by-chapter loop with visible progress — hours of grunt work off your desk, every judgment call still yours.

I just finished a full self-edit of my latest manuscript — now I'm ready for human eyes on it.

Perfect timing, honestly. Run the Clinic before the human eyes. Two reasons:

Money: human editors bill for the pass they perform. If they spend it circling filter words and continuity slips a scanner could have caught, you paid professional rates for mechanical cleanup. Hand them a clinic-cleaned manuscript and their expensive attention goes where it's actually needed — the story.

Communication: the downloadable Manuscript Report — verdict, priorities, charts, before/after fixes — doubles as a briefing document. Give it to your editor or beta readers and they know exactly where the book stands and what you already addressed.

Other languages
My book is written in Spanish. My AI keeps losing continuity between chapters and flagging "errors" the manuscript already explains. Can this handle Spanish — and actually keep my characters, relationships, and plot details straight?

Yes — Spanish is one of the Clinic's built-in languages (with French, German, Italian, and Brazilian Portuguese). Choose it once and every editing prompt instructs your AI to analyze the Spanish text as Spanish — replying in Spanish, respecting Spanish dialogue and typography conventions, and hunting for Spanish AI-tells rather than pattern-matching English ones.

On continuity — your real complaint — the Clinic attacks it three ways: a dedicated continuity-and-timeline pass builds a story log across the whole manuscript; the character report tracks who appears in every chapter and flags anyone who silently vanishes; and because the tool works chapter by chapter with paste-back, every prompt is built from your newest text — so the AI stops flagging "errors" you already fixed two drafts ago.

One honest caveat, because honesty is the whole point of this sheet: the instant scans and charts — the word-list flag counts and graphs — are tuned for English, so on a Spanish manuscript treat them as rough signals, and lean on the editing passes (which do the real editorial work, fully in Spanish) for the verdicts that matter. The built-in Spanish is currently tuned to European/Castilian conventions — tell us if you write Latin-American Spanish. Authors editing in Spanish helped us pressure-test all of this, and deeper Spanish support is already on the roadmap.

My English is good enough to read, but not to write — I assumed that counted me out.

You're not counted out — the Clinic meets you in your language. Set the manuscript language and the editing conversation happens entirely there: the prompts order your AI to write every note, every example, and every fix in your language. Your English only needs to survive the tool's buttons, and the step-by-step guide names the exact button to click at every step.
