Lesson 4: Pass 3 — Timeline Consistency

Because nothing pulls a reader out faster than a Tuesday that happens twice

01 The Prompt

Copy this prompt and paste it into your AI tool with your timeline log (if you have one) and chapter text.

Timeline Consistency Prompt
You are editing a chapter for [PEN NAME], a [GENRE] author. Here is the story timeline log so far (if available): [PASTE TIMELINE LOG OR "No prior log — build from this chapter"] Here is the chapter to check: [PASTE FULL CHAPTER HERE] This is Chapter [NUMBER]. The previous chapter ended on [DAY/TIME/LOCATION if known]. Run a TIMELINE CONSISTENCY CHECK on this chapter. Check all 8 points: 1. SCENE DURATION — How long does this scene take in story time? Is that consistent with what characters accomplish? 2. TIME ANCHORS — Does this chapter reference a day, date, time of day, or elapsed time? Log it. Does it match the previous chapter? 3. TRAVEL & LOGISTICS — If characters move locations, does the travel time make sense? 4. SEASON/WEATHER/LIGHT — Does the described light, temperature, or weather match the established season and time of day? 5. CHARACTER FATIGUE — Do characters show appropriate fatigue, hunger, recovery based on elapsed story time? 6. CONCURRENT EVENTS — If events are happening simultaneously in different locations, do they track? 7. RECENCY OF REFERENCE — When characters reference past events ("last week," "two days ago"), does the math work? 8. AGE & LIFE-STAGE — If ages or timeframes are referenced, do they track with earlier chapters? Output: Build or update a STORY TIMELINE LOG in this format: [Chapter X] Day N | Time of Day | Location | Key Events | Time Elapsed FLAGS: [TIMELINE GAP] / [POSSIBLE CONTRADICTION] / [CONFIRM WITH AUTHOR]
[TIMELINE GAP] [POSSIBLE CONTRADICTION] [CONFIRM WITH AUTHOR]

TIMELINE GAP = missing time not accounted for. POSSIBLE CONTRADICTION = reference conflicts with established timeline. CONFIRM WITH AUTHOR = ambiguity that needs your decision.

Example Timeline Log Output:
[Ch. 1] Day 1 | Morning | Maple Creek, Emma's bakery | Emma opens shop, meets Jack for first time | Story start [Ch. 2] Day 1 | Afternoon | Town square, then Emma's apartment | Festival setup, Emma sees Jack again | ~6 hours [Ch. 3] Day 2 | Morning | Emma's bakery, then Jack's ranch | Emma delivers order, sees ranch for first time | ~18 hours

02 Before & After Example

Timeline reference error: character uses "last Tuesday" but the established timeline shows it was Thursday (3 days ago, not 5).

Before
[POSSIBLE CONTRADICTION] Ch. 9
"When I talked to him last Tuesday, he mentioned the property sale," Clara said, wrapping her hands around her mug.
Timeline log shows the conversation with Ben occurred on Day 12 (Thursday). Current scene is Day 15 (Sunday). "Last Tuesday" would be Day 10, which is 2 days before the conversation actually happened. The math doesn't work.
After
Fixed
"When I talked to him a few days ago, he mentioned the property sale," Clara said, wrapping her hands around her mug.
Changed to a vague-but-accurate reference. "A few days ago" covers the 3-day gap without creating a verifiable math error. Alternative fix: change to "on Thursday" for precision.
Pro Tip

When you find timeline reference errors, you have two fix strategies. Precise: use the correct day name or date. Vague: use relative language ("a few days ago," "earlier this week") that's accurate without being checkable. Both work. Choose based on whether the specific day matters to your story.

Workflow Reminder

Carry your timeline log forward with every chapter. Start with Chapter 1 (no prior log). After each chapter, copy the updated log into the next chapter's prompt. By the end, you'll have a complete story timeline document. Keep it for continuity checks during future books in a series.
