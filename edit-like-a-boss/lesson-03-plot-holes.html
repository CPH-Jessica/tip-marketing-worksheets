Find the cracks before you paint the walls

01 The Prompt

Copy this prompt and paste it into your AI tool along with your codex, outline, and chapter text.

Plot Hole Checker Prompt
You are editing a chapter for [PEN NAME], a [GENRE] author. Heat level: [1-5]/5. Here is the codex/story bible for this project: [PASTE CODEX HERE] Here is the chapter outline showing where this chapter sits in the arc: [PASTE OUTLINE HERE] Here is the chapter to check: [PASTE FULL CHAPTER HERE] Run a PLOT HOLE CHECK on this chapter. Check all 8 points: 1. CAUSE & EFFECT — Does everything in this chapter flow logically from what came before? Flag any action or reaction that lacks a clear cause. 2. SETUP TRACKING — Does this chapter introduce a promise (a secret, an object, a character decision, a foreshadowed event) that will need to pay off? Log it. 3. PAYOFF TRACKING — Does this chapter resolve a setup from an earlier chapter? Confirm the payoff matches the setup's weight. 4. CHARACTER KNOWLEDGE — Is every character operating only on information they've actually received in-story? Flag any character who acts on knowledge they couldn't have. 5. LOGICAL CHARACTER BEHAVIOR — Do characters make decisions consistent with their established personality, wounds, and goals? Flag any moment where someone acts "because the plot needs it." 6. INTRODUCED-BUT-DROPPED — Does this chapter mention something the narrative then ignores? 7. CONTRADICTIONS — Any fact that conflicts with an established fact from earlier chapters? 8. OFF-PAGE LOGIC — Are there events implied to have happened off-page that strain believability? Output format: List flags as [PLOT HOLE], [SETUP LOGGED], [PAYOFF CHECK], or [WATCH]. Include chapter location. Then provide a summary of critical issues vs. watch items.
[PLOT HOLE] [SETUP LOGGED] [PAYOFF CHECK] [WATCH]

PLOT HOLE = broken, fix it. SETUP LOGGED = promise made, track it. PAYOFF CHECK = promise resolved, verify weight. WATCH = worth attention, use judgment.

02 Before & After Example

Character knowledge violation: a character acts on information they were never given on-page.

Before
[PLOT HOLE] Ch. 8
"I know what happened in Denver," Lila said softly, setting down her coffee cup.
Lila was not present in Ch. 6 when Jake told Marcus about Denver. No scene establishes how she learned this. Character is acting on information she doesn't have.
After
Fixed
"Marcus called me last night." Lila set down her coffee cup, her fingers tightening around it. "He told me what happened in Denver."
Information bridge established. Lila received the information through a plausible on-page channel (Marcus called her). Knowledge flow is now trackable.
Pro Tip

You have three fix options for character knowledge violations: add a bridge scene where they learn the info, change the dialogue so they're guessing or bluffing, or plant an earlier scene where someone lets it slip. Pick the one that serves your story best.
