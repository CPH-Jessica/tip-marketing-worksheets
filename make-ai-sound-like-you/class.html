The method — do each step with me, leave with the artifact

How to make AI sound like you

Module 1

Mine your voice

You can't tell AI to sound like you until you know what "you" actually sounds like.

The move: Pull 3–5 samples of your own real writing you're proud of. We find your patterns — sentence rhythm, the moves you make, your humor, the words you reach for, the things you'd never say.
Do this: Paste your samples into Claude and ask it to describe your voice back to you as a set of rules. You react: "yes, that's me / no, that's not." You'll have: a first draft of your Voice Profile.
Module 2

Feed receipts, not adjectives

"Write it warm and funny" gets you everyone's warm and funny. Specifics get you YOURS.

The move: Generic inputs make generic output. The fix is your real, lived, specific material — the details only you have. That's what makes a page unmistakably you and impossible for a generic AI writer to fake.
Do this: Build a Receipts Bank — your real stories, phrases, failures, references, the things that actually happened. You'll have: a file of specifics you drop in so the AI writes from your life, not the internet's average.
Module 3

Build your banned list

Half of sounding like you is refusing to sound like the robot.

The move: Name the AI tells and kill them on sight — the em-dash overload, the "it's not X, it's Y" reflex, the dramatic three-word triplets, the tidy 5-point listicle skeleton, the fortune-cookie endings. Plus the words and phrases that simply aren't you.
Do this: We build your personal banned list together. You'll have: a Banned List you paste in every time so the AI stops defaulting to wallpaper.
Module 4

Pick your register before you write

You don't have one voice — you have a few. Choose on purpose.

The move: Are you the expert who knows, or the friend who gets it? Teaching voice and personal-story voice are different instruments. The biggest mistake is blending them. We learn to pick first, then write.
Do this: Define your 2 main registers and when each one comes out. You'll have: a one-line "which voice, when" rule.
Module 5

Direct, don't accept

This is the whole game. You're the director, not the typist.

The move: You make the calls — the hook, the angle, the structure, the standard. The AI drafts to your direction. Then you run the keep-it-or-cut-it loop on every line. Generic? Back. Wrong? Gone. Nothing ships that isn't yours.
Do this: Take one AI draft and we redirect it live until it sounds like you. You'll have: the director's loop as muscle memory.
Module 6

Protect the trust (don't let it lie for you)

Sounding like you is worthless if it makes things up in your name.

The move: AI will hand you a confident, beautiful, completely fabricated detail and not blink. We set the rule: the boring true version beats the sharp invented one, every time. How to catch fabrication before it ships under your name.
Do this: Learn the verify-before-you-publish pass. You'll have: a short pre-publish checklist that keeps your reader trust intact.
Module 7

Use the right tool for the job

No single AI is best at everything. Knowing the difference is part of the craft.

The move: I'll show you my actual stack and when I reach for which tool, so you build instinct for matching the job to the right model instead of forcing one to do everything.
Do this: Map your own tasks to tools. You'll have: a simple routing cheat sheet.
The standard we hold the whole class

The one test that decides everything

"Could a stranger tell whether I wrote this, or the AI did?"

If they couldn't tell — it's working. Ship it. If it reads clean, smooth, tidy, and generic — that's the robot, and it goes back. Every technique in this class exists to pass that one test. We run it on your real writing until passing it is automatic.
