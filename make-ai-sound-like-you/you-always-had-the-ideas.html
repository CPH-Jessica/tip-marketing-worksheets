What this class is

The book was always good. I just couldn't finish it.

I wrote a romance in 2011. I published it. And I always knew it wasn't the book it could be. I knew exactly what was wrong with it. I even fixed a lot of it, twice, on my own. What I couldn't do — for years — was sit still long enough to finish the hardest rewrite.

This class is the proof of what changed when I stopped fighting that and let AI take over the part I struggle with. I'll show you all three versions of the same book, side by side, so you can see the evolution with your own eyes — and decide for yourself whether the woman who directed that rebuild is any less the author.

Spoiler: she's not. And neither are you.

The thing holding you back

Let's kill the myth first

The myth

"If you use AI, you're not really a writer. The AI wrote it. You just pushed a button. Real authors do it themselves."

It's the fear that keeps so many of us — especially over 40 and 50 — standing at the edge of this, afraid to even try.

The truth

The words were never the work. The story is the work. The judgment, the taste, the knowing what a reader needs and when — that's the author. That's you.

AI can put words on a page. It cannot know your reader, your voice, or what your book is supposed to feel like. Only you know that.

The proof — one book, three drafts, eleven years

Watch a book (and a writer) grow up

Same love story every time: teenage sweethearts torn apart by a betrayal, a pregnant woman in danger, the detective who never stopped loving her, and a serial killer. Here's how it evolved.

Stage 1 — written by me, 2011

Unexpected Destinies

My debut era. All me, no AI.
  • Opens on a goddess prologue — literal gods run the plot from a magic viewing pool.
  • The exciting part (a woman running for her life) is buried, then cut to a flashback.
  • Head-hopping, telling instead of showing, the love story driven by divine meddling instead of the characters.
↓
Stage 2 — revised by me, pre-AI

Forgiven Destinies

Years later. Still all me. Still no AI.
  • I moved the hook — cut the gods from the opening, started on the detective at a crime scene.
  • I fixed the head-hopping: one point of view per chapter.
  • I polished the prose and sharpened the comedy. This is the receipt that the craft was always mine.
  • But I couldn't finish the hardest cuts — the gods stayed in, the book stayed unfocused. Not for lack of knowing. For lack of finishing.
↓
Stage 3 — directed by me, written with Claude, 2025

Model Behavior  — published

I made every call. AI executed them to my standard.
  • Cut the gods entirely — the characters finally drive their own story.
  • Repositioned the heroine into a self-made billionaire CEO; gave the hero real stature to match.
  • Unified the plot — the old betrayal and the present-day killer became one decade-long obsession. The early drafts never connected those threads. This one does.
  • Removed a scene that no longer held up, and replaced the whole secret-baby setup with a haunting recurring nightmare that pays off in the ending.
Three drafts. A decade apart. One unmistakable storyteller. The ideas, the diagnosis, the standard — mine the whole way. The tool didn't make me a writer. It helped me finish becoming the one I already was.
The honest part

It was never a talent problem. It was a finishing problem.

Here's what I don't usually say out loud: my brain runs a hundred miles an hour. I can map a whole series in an afternoon. I have more ideas than I'll ever live to write. What I struggle with — what my ADD makes genuinely hard — is sitting still long enough to grind out the finish.

For years I blamed myself for that. Called myself lazy, undisciplined, a starter who couldn't finish. Maybe you've called yourself those things too.

We were wrong. It's not a character flaw. It's wiring. And the right tool doesn't fix your wiring — it works with it. I bring the vision and the judgment. Claude brings the focus and the follow-through I can't always summon. I'm not handing over my book. I'm handing over the bottleneck.

The reframe that changes everything:

AI isn't a ghostwriter. It's a finisher you direct. For an idea-rich, focus-poor writer, that's not cheating. That's the assistive tool that finally lets the book out of your head.
