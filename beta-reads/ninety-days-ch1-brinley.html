FIRST HIT

Gut reaction

Okay. OKAY. Connie. I sat down braced for a quiet little farm thing, and within ONE PAGE a six-foot dinosaur bird had stolen the legal paperwork and I was already screaming into a throw pillow. I'm obsessed.

Our girl kicked off her heels and chased a felony emu through an orchard in bare feet, in a skirt, and won. That has guts. I read the whole thing in one sitting and immediately wanted Chapter 2, which is, like, the entire job of a Chapter 1. Job done.

The vibe I walked away with: spiky city chef versus a man-shaped brick wall, ninety-day clock already ticking, and I am SO ready to watch her crack him open. These are romcom bones, babe. Grumpy / sunshine, except the sunshine is a little feral and carries knives. I'm in.

HEAT REPORT

Priority one

I have to be real with you because you told me this is a 4/5, and I'm holding you to it.

There's no heat on the page in Chapter 1, and that's completely correct. Nobody's getting naked in the gravel next to a goat pen. It's the meet. So I'm not grading heat, I'm grading the promise. Does this chapter make me trust the spice is coming?

Right now... mostly not yet. And it's a one-beat fix. Elena clocks Silas: broad shoulders, angles and stubble, green flecks in the eyes, the set jaw. Good details. But she reads him like she's sizing up a rival line cook, not like a single nerve in her body woke up. There's zero flicker of want under the antagonism.

For a 4/5, I need one beat in this chapter where she notices something about him and HATES that she noticed. The forearms. That voice "that didn't need volume to fill space" (that line is right there and doing nothing for me yet, push it). Half a second where she's mad her stomach did a thing. That single beat is the contract you sign with a heat reader. Plant it and I'll trust you for the whole ninety days. Leave it out and Chapter 1 reads clean, which is going to confuse the reader who bought it for the 4.

WORKING

Stuff that hit

  • The emu. Beatrix. PERFECT name. "You are not getting paid for this shift" made me physically put my phone down. Screenshot. Group chat. Done.
  • Elena's voice. "I don't quit on day one." "I have good hearing when people are trying to scare me off." Competent, spiky, doesn't fold. I'd follow her into battle. I'd follow her into a Costco.
  • The closing banter. "Goats need moving. You'll want boots." / "I want a lot of things. Boots are a start." THAT is the romcom crackle. That's the engine. I want that energy threaded through everything.
  • "Maggie's idea." "The name landed and sat there. He didn't explain it." I gasped. Who is Maggie?? That's a hook with teeth. Do not resolve it for ten chapters, I'm begging.
  • Silas talks in verdicts, not questions ("You the chef."). Great character tic. He speaks in convictions. Keep that, it's doing a lot of quiet work.
NEEDS WORK

Where she lost me a little

The heat-promise beat. Big one, see above. One sentence fixes it.

Silas needs one hairline crack. The wall is correct, but I need to believe there's a person worth ninety days under the flannel. You already gave me the seed: "his mouth twitched. Not a smile. Something closer to surprise." THAT is the crack. Give me maybe one more flicker like it so the wall reads as a locked door, not a solid wall. Locked doors are sexy. Solid walls are just walls.

Marlene's phone call. It does a lot of rules-explaining and stops the scene a touch to do it. The ninety-day setup HAS to land, so it earns its place, but right now it reads a little like a contract being read aloud (because it is). Let her personality carry more of it. She's already got it ("she likes shiny things and people who look like they don't belong"), so lean there and it'll feel like a character instead of a clause.

MIGHT STING

The real-talk part

If I didn't already know this was Connie and a 4/5, I'd have guessed clean small-town. The writing's genuinely good and the voice is great, but the romance temperature in Chapter 1 reads sweet, not simmering.

And here's the thing: that's not a prose problem. It's a one-beat problem. The fix is NOT "add a sex scene to the meet" (god, no). The fix is to let Elena's body betray her exactly once. That's the whole difference between a reader thinking "aw, cute" and a reader thinking "oh, these two are going to be a PROBLEM for each other." Problem is what sells the four.
