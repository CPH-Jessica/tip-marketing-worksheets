The path — each lesson has a worksheet you actually fill in

How the class runs

Part 1

Set it up right

Ten minutes here saves you months of fighting it later.

You'll cover: The account setup checklist — what to turn on, what to ignore, and how the pieces fit together. Then the setup test, so you can prove it's working instead of hoping.
You'll have: a Claude that's configured on purpose, plus a troubleshooting page for the day it starts acting strange.
Part 2

Teach Claude who you are

This is the step everybody skips, and it's the one that changes everything.

You'll cover: Building your author profile — your genres, your pen names, your habits, your standards, how you like to be talked to. Plus the MBTI-to-Claude translator, for turning "how I actually think" into instructions Claude can follow.
You'll have: a written author profile you paste in once and reuse forever.
Part 3

Skills — read one, build one, test it

A Skill is how you stop re-teaching Claude the same job every time.

You'll cover: The Skill Anatomy Explorer — what the parts of a Skill are and why they're there. Then you build: a Voice Skill that writes like you, a Beta Reader, and an Editor. Then the Skill Testing Lab, where you find out whether yours actually works before you trust it on a manuscript.
You'll have: your own working Skills — voice, beta reader, editor — tested, not theoretical.
Part 4

Projects — give the work a home

A Project is the room your book lives in. Build the room before you move in.

You'll cover: The Project Architecture Explorer — how to decide what goes where. The Project Instructions Builder — writing the standing orders Claude follows every time you open it. Plus the Cowork Projects workbook: understand it, build it, move into it, run it.
You'll have: project instructions written and loaded, so Claude opens already knowing the rules.
Part 5

Set up a book — or a whole series

One book and a nine-book series are not the same build.

You'll cover: Single Book Project Setup for a standalone, and the Series Project Planner for everything that has to stay consistent across a shelf — continuity, cast, timeline, and what carries forward.
You'll have: a project structure that fits what you're actually writing.
Part 6

Plan the book, in the right order

Premise, then codex, then outline, then chapter prompts. Skip a step and the middle collapses.

You'll cover: The 5 Core Elements and your Story Promise — what you're promising the reader and whether you're keeping it. The Premise & Codex Builder. The Outline Builder. Then the Plot Hole Check and chapter prompts, so you go into drafting with the cracks already found.
You'll have: a premise, a codex, an outline, and chapter prompts — for a real book, not a sample one.
Companion tools

The two you'll keep reaching for

Included with the class.

You'll get: The Character Tracker, for keeping a cast straight across a book or a series. And the Pen Name Skill Builder, for spinning up a fresh Skill the next time you start a new name.
You'll have: tools that outlast the class — they work on the next book too.
