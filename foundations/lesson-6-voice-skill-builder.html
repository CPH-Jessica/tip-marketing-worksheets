1 Pen Name & Genre

The basics — who is this voice for and where does it sit on the shelf?

The name on the book cover.
Be specific. "Romance" is too broad. What kind?
Tip: Think about where a reader would find your book in a store. "Paranormal rom-com with vampires" is better than "paranormal romance." The more specific your genre description, the better Claude matches reader expectations.

2 Heat Level

How spicy does this pen name get? The number matters less than the description.

3 Sensual — on-page but not explicit
What happens on-page? What stays behind closed doors? What words do you use or avoid?
Tip: Your "3" is different from every other author's "3." The description is what Claude actually uses — the number is just a quick reference. Be honest about what your readers expect.

4 Signature Moves

The things a reader would recognize as yours even without your name on the cover.

One per line. What makes your prose distinctly yours?
Not sure? Open your best-reviewed book. Read the passages readers highlighted or quoted in reviews. Those are your signature moves — the things that made someone stop and think "this is why I read this author."

5 POV & Structure

6 Dialogue Style

How your characters talk is where voice lives or dies.

Fast or slow? Quippy or deliberate? Contractions? Dialect? Profanity?
Tip: Read your dialogue out loud. If it sounds like two people actually talking, you're in the right zone. If it sounds like narration wearing quotation marks, you need to loosen it up.
