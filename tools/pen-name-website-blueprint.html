The Pen Name Website Blueprint

How to get a real website — your own domain, email, even a database — without writing a single line of code. You direct; your AI collaborator builds. I did this exact process for a reader community in three days, and this is the map.

Let's begin

Who this is for: you have a pen name (or a reader group, or a class, or an idea) that deserves its own home on the internet — and every "website builder" you've tried wants $20+ a month to hold your content hostage on their platform.

The honest promise: by the end of this walkthrough you'll have a real website at your own address (like yourpenname.com), email at that address, and a setup where changes cost you a sentence — "add a page for my new series" — instead of an afternoon.

What it costs: about $11 a year for the domain name. Everything else in this blueprint has a genuinely usable free tier as of July 2026. Monthly bill: $0.

The one rule that makes this work

You and your AI collaborator (I use Claude — in Claude Code or Cowork, where it can actually do things, not just chat) split the work like this:

🖋 Only YOU ever do

Create accounts · type passwords · click "Authorize" buttons · enter payment info · approve anything that publishes or spends money

🤖 Claude does everything else

  • Writes all the code
  • Connects all the services
  • Sets up the technical records
  • Tests everything before you touch it
  • Fixes what breaks

Never give your passwords to the AI. Ever. A good collaborator will stop, hand you the keyboard for the password moment, and pick the work back up after. If it asks you to paste a password into chat — that's your cue to say no. (Claude is built to refuse this anyway. Let it.)

Two levels — most pen name sites only need the first:

Level 1 — receiving (free, built into Porkbun): hello@yourpenname.com forwards into the Gmail you already check. Looks professional on your back matter and newsletter; costs nothing; takes five minutes.

Level 2 — sending (free tier at resend.com): only if your SITE needs to send mail — welcome emails when someone joins your newsletter, alerts to you when someone submits a form. This is the fanciest wiring in the blueprint, and it's still just: you create the account, Claude sets up the records, you paste one key.

🤖 Claude does

Patience moment: internet address changes (DNS) can take minutes to a few hours to spread worldwide. If something doesn't work immediately after a records change, wait twenty minutes before worrying. Claude will keep checking for you — mine literally watches until it goes green.

When things go sideways (they will, briefly)

Every one of these happened in my real build. None of them mattered by dinnertime.

"I made the account but Claude's browser shows me logged out"
You're probably signed in on a different browser window or profile than the one Claude is driving. Sign in on the window Claude names — or tell Claude which window YOU'RE in and let it reconnect there. (I run three Chrome windows. This cost us twenty minutes and a small mystery involving my laptop being closed.)
"We changed a setting and nothing happened"
DNS propagation — address changes take minutes to hours to spread. Wait 20 minutes. Also: some registrar dashboards have TWO settings screens where one silently fails — if a record "won't save," ask Claude to verify from the outside (it can check what the world actually sees) rather than trusting the screen.
"The site shows old information after a change"
Two suspects: your browser cached the page (hard-refresh: Ctrl+Shift+R), or the site itself is caching data it should refetch — that one's a real bug; tell Claude "the page shows stale data but the underlying info is right" and it'll know exactly where to look. Mine did this with new book submissions on day three; fixed in one line.
"A scary warning popped up mid-setup"
Read it to Claude verbatim before clicking anything. Half the time it's expected (ours warned that switching mail routing would disconnect the old forwarding — which was the entire plan). The other half, Claude will tell you to stop. Either way: the warning is information, not an emergency.
"I lost track of which account does what"
GitHub = the code's home. Vercel = makes it a live website. Porkbun = your name + email forwarding. Supabase = remembers things. Resend = sends email. Ask Claude to write YOUR version of this map with your actual links after the build — mine keeps one updated for me and it has saved me a dozen times.
