Step 2 · The Narrative Engine

Engine & Series Promise

A narrative engine is the core premise that naturally keeps generating stories. External engines = a place or institution (small town, crime family, royal court, MC charter). Internal engines = a shared bond (a bloodline curse, a childhood pact, collective trauma). A strong engine answers: "Why does this world keep producing books?"

Step 3 · The emotional argument

Overarching Theme

Theme is the emotional argument of your series — the truth your characters prove or disprove across every book. Ask: what universal human experience are they wrestling with? Then map it macro → micro: the whole series moves from one emotional state to another, and each book explores a different facet of it.

Macro arc — where the world/group starts and lands

Micro arc — each book is a facet of the theme

e.g. if the theme is overcoming trauma: Book 1 = denial, Book 2 = anger, Book 3 = acceptance. Fill one facet per book.

Step 6 · Reader promises

Element & Trope Bible

Tropes aren't clichés — they're reader promises. Pick your recurring tropes (the series' signature, constant across books) here; assign each book its unique tropes on the Book Cards tab. Sweet spot: 3–5 recurring, plus 1–3 unique per book. More than that muddies the promise.

Step 7 · The cast + the pipeline

Characters & Side-Character Pipeline

This is where series money lives. Track your leads and the side characters you're seeding as future leads (the breadcrumb technique). For each, note their first impression, secret wound, future payoff, world role, and which book pays them off. Introduce side characters as future emotional assets, never randomly.

Step 8 · The payoff engine

Threading Board

The whole point of planning a series: plant in one book, harvest in another. Don't plant random cool details — plant details you intend to pay off. This board tracks promises, secrets, breadcrumbs, and the escalating betrayal ladder across every book.

Step 9 · Book by book

Book Cards

Each book gets the 5-column map (emotional payoff · external payoff · major reveal · next-book hook) plus its four endings and its "why now?" urgency. Rule: each book closes a door and opens a bigger one. The final book resolves — everyone else re-hooks.
