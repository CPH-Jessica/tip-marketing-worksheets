THE INVISIBLE PEN · LIVE TALK TRACK · 25–35 MIN

Run the newsletter without living in the inbox

Demo author on screen: Maisie Quill, small-town romance, MailerLite. Not your house bots. Not Karen. Not Gunther.

0:00 · Open 2 min · title slide

Babe, listen. A newsletter bot is not a tiny employee with a Send key. It’s a draft mule with a calendar. Today you leave with one bot, one routine, and the Send button still in your hand.

0:02 · Meet the crew 3 min · slide 2

Draft writes. Gate is you. Monday runs while you’re at the store. Inbox only gets one owner. If two bots sit in the same inbox, this is your face by Thursday.

0:05 · Chat vs bot 3 min

Need one subject line punched up? Stay in chat. Need the same list, same Tuesday, same voice, still there next month? That’s a bot. A chat ends when you close the tab. A bot ends when the routine is saved.

0:08 · One inbox 3 min

If you already have an email bot, you are not making a second one called Weekly Recap Fairy. Duplicate bots linger. They argue over the draft. Name it Newsletter. Boring on purpose.
Swaps are a different job. Do not make Draft hunt BookFunnel. Your personal essay will start selling someone else’s book 3.

0:11 · Standing purpose 4 min · read the paste block out loud

This paragraph is the job title for the rest of its life. This week’s issue does not go in here. Send does not go in here. If the description is a grocery list, the bot will shop forever.
On screen: paste the formula paragraph into Maisie’s bot description. Point at “You never send.”

0:15 · The gate 4 min · tell the receipt

I sent a fabricated blurb because I trusted a model to know a book it had not read. That is the whole argument for the human gate. The bot may draft, stage, suggest three subject lines. It does not press Send. Set-and-forget is how that story happens again.

0:19 · Voice samples 3 min

Three real issues. Not the pretty one. The Tuesday one. Without those, it writes the internet’s author newsletter — smooth, dead, could belong to anybody.
Paste two short paragraphs from a fake Maisie issue. Show the difference if you have time: no sample vs sample.

0:22 · Routines 4 min

A routine is a prompt plus a trigger. Monday nine, draft. Friday three, recap. Both end with leave it here. Two routines is a system. Twelve is a second job.
Save the Monday prompt on the Maisie bot. Do not run a live send. Watch it draft if the room is patient. Stop at the draft.

0:26 · Failure modes 3 min

Week two blowups: two bots, purpose stuffed with a preorder that already closed, no voice sample, “just send it,” swaps inside the same bot, invented open rates. If you didn’t paste the number, it does not go in the letter.

0:29 · Homework + close 3–6 min

Done means five things: bot or reuse, purpose pasted with no Send, three issues in the thread, Monday saved, one unsent draft. Post the purpose paragraph and three subject lines. Ross will tell you if it’s a job or a grocery list.
The mule drafts. You still hit Send. That’s the class.

Q&A traps

  • Can it send if I say so once? No. The gate is standing. One-time permission becomes a habit.
  • Substack vs MailerLite? Same job. Different buttons. Connect the service the list actually lives in. Don’t invent a connector you don’t have.
  • Can Ross be my newsletter bot? No. Ross is classroom. Newsletter is a different job.
  • Prices / which Grok plan? That’s Jessica’s call. Don’t quote plans you haven’t checked today.
