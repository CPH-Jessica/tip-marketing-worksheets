Worksheet 06 / 18

Plan for your actual week

The five classes provide 225 protected work minutes in total: 180 minutes in the four classes before the November 4 draft target, plus 45 minutes in the November 5 final class. Add only the outside work blocks you can realistically protect.

Drafting estimate: intended draft words minus words already written = words remaining. Divide by the number of planned work blocks. Example only: 18,000 words over 24 blocks = 750 words per block. Compare blocks of similar length; this is a planning check, not a quota.

If the pace does not fit, reduce scope or make a specific completion plan with Jessica. Word counts can fall during good revision. Minutes, decisions, and repaired scenes count.

Worksheet 13 / 18

When the writing gets stuck

Pick one small move. You do not have to solve the whole novella to make useful progress.

Blank page | 5 minutes

Write: "My character wants ___, but ___, so they choose ___." Draft only the next exchange. Leave a research placeholder and keep going.

Stalled middle | 5 minutes

List three possible consequences of the last choice. Pick one and write its first paragraph.

Revision fog | 10 minutes

Save the original. Read one paragraph aloud. Mark where meaning or emotion blurs, then revise only that passage.

Low-energy day | 5 minutes

Outline three scene beats, dictate a paragraph using a tool you already have, or write for five minutes and reassess.

Missed class or interrupted week

Read that class worksheet. Do one 20-minute work block, or a smaller workable step. Bring one question next time. Adjust the plan instead of punishing yourself with twice the homework.
