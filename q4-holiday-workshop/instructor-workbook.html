Voluntary Screen-Sharing Coaching

Offer, do not require: “Would you like to share one relevant window and one question? You can also read a short passage, paste an excerpt you are comfortable sharing in chat, describe the problem, or keep writing while I use my example.” Participation, cameras, and word-count sharing are optional.

Before a writer shares

Three five-minute turns in the 15-minute coaching window

  1. 1 minute: the writer states the intended effect and one obstacle.
  2. 3 minutes: Jessica asks one diagnostic question, offers one or two possible moves, and demonstrates a small change with permission.
  3. 1 minute: the writer chooses the move to try and names the next action. Keep their voice and choices intact.

Invite volunteers at check-in. Prioritize people who have not had a turn, rotate across the five weeks, and limit each person to one turn per class. If more than three volunteer, keep an optional carry-over queue; do not promise everyone an individual turn each week. Others can keep writing. No volunteers? Use the prepared example. Park complex questions for the next relevant lesson.

Useful coaching language“What do you want the reader to feel here? Which sentence is already doing that? Would you like to try one small change, then read it back?” Ask permission before rewriting; the author decides what to keep.

No AI account is required. If a writer chooses AI suggestions, use only material they are comfortable sharing with that tool; the author checks accuracy, fit, and voice and can reject any suggestion.

Weekly Progress & Next-Step Commitments

At the start: “What moved, what got in the way, and what will you work on today?” At the end: “I worked on __. I changed or wrote __. My next small step is __, during __.” Writers may keep answers private or share in chat. Minutes worked and revision decisions count.

Use this as a demonstration or Jessica's brief teaching log. Students keep their own copy. Notes stay in this browser, so keep a separate copy of anything you need; avoid confidential student material.

When someone is stuck or behind

  • Blank-page start (5 minutes): write “My character wants __, but __, so they choose __.” Draft only the next exchange; use a placeholder for research.
  • Middle-of-story reset (5 minutes): list three possible consequences of the last choice; pick one and write its first paragraph.
  • Revision alternative (10 minutes): read one paragraph aloud, mark where the meaning or emotion blurs, and revise that passage. Keep a copy of the original.
  • Low-energy option: outline three scene beats, dictate a paragraph in an already available tool, or work for five minutes and reassess. No new software is needed.
  • Missed class: read the session's outcome, do one 20-minute work block, and bring one question next time. Use a class recording if Jessica has made one available; only the October 8 replay is currently promised on signup.
Emergency interruption planIf a technical problem leaves only 60 minutes: 5 minutes check-in, 5 minutes teaching, 20 minutes writing, 10 minutes coaching (two volunteers), 15 minutes applying the feedback, 5 minutes commitments. The scheduled class remains 90 minutes; use this only for an unexpected interruption, keeping the outcome small and protecting both work blocks.

❓ Q&A Cheat Sheet

“Is October too late to start?”

We are working toward a Christmas novella draft by November 4. Start with the material you have, scope the novella to your available time, and check your pace after a sprint. Each author chooses a release date that allows for editing, proofreading, formatting, and the remaining production work.

“What is the draft target?”

November 4, including our final class. Aim for a complete Christmas novella draft; early finishers can revise. If you are behind, bring the draft you have and a clear list of remaining scenes so Jessica can help you make a completion plan. Your release date is your own choice.

“Do I need all three seasonal books?”

No. These five weeks focus on one Christmas novella. Keep additional seasonal projects for another plan.

“Can I just put a wreath on a regular book?”

Look for a real story connection: a holiday deadline, tradition, obligation, or weather event that changes a choice. Try removing the season and ask what would need to change in the plot.

“Do we all release on the same date?”

No. Each author chooses their own release date after allowing time for revision, editing, proofreading, formatting, cover work, and platform review. The shared course target is a Christmas novella draft by November 4; there is no shared upload deadline.

“What if I cannot share my screen or use the toolkit?”

Use a document or paper for every writing task. You can describe the issue, read aloud, share a short excerpt voluntarily, or work privately while Jessica demonstrates. Follow the published access terms for the premium interactive toolkit.

Premium members do not need to purchase; their joining instructions are coming soon. Direct questions to Jessica; do not treat an email request as enrollment or payment.

“Who gets the replay?”
