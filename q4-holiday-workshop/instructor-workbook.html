❓ Q&A Cheat Sheet

“Is it too late to write a Christmas book?”

No — it's July. A 25–30k novella drafted in August, edited in September, released November 1–10 is comfortably on schedule. It's too late in October. That's the whole point of tonight.

“Do holiday books die after the holiday?”

They hibernate, they don't die. They come back every single year with zero extra work — a holiday book is an annuity. Plus KU readers binge them all season.

“Can I just put a wreath on the cover of a regular book?”

Readers can smell it. The wrapper has to do plot work — if you can delete the holiday and the story survives, seasonal readers will one-star the costume.

“Which wave sells best?”

Christmas, by a mile — but it's also the most crowded. Spooky and cozy autumn have thinner shelves, so a well-positioned book stands out faster. Best answer: the wave that fits your existing catalog and voice.

“What if I miss my window?”

Decide on purpose: either compress scope (novella, not novel) or slide to next year and be the first preorder of NEXT season. The only wrong move is releasing December 23rd to silence.

“Do I need all three books?”

Absolutely not. One well-marketed holiday release with a real runway beats three rushed ones. The 3-book run is for authors with a novella pipeline already humming.
