The Copyright Page Template

The page that goes right after your title page. It tells the world the book is yours, protects your rights, and gives Amazon and readers the legal details they expect. Copy the version that fits your book, swap in your details, done.

How to use this: Start with the Standard Template below — it works for almost every book. Everything highlighted like [ THIS ] is a blank for you to fill in. Then check the Variations for any extra lines that match your situation (KU, wide, pen name, AI tools). You can paste this straight into Vellum, Word, or your KDP manuscript file. The copyright page is almost always the second page of your book, before the dedication.

Standard Copyright Page

Start here

Covers fiction for nearly all indie authors. If you only use one template, use this one.

[ BOOK TITLE ]

Copyright © [ YEAR ] by [ AUTHOR / PEN NAME ]

• • •

All rights reserved. No part of this book may be reproduced, distributed, or transmitted in any form or by any means, including photocopying, recording, or other electronic or mechanical methods, without the prior written permission of the author, except in the case of brief quotations embodied in critical reviews and certain other noncommercial uses permitted by copyright law.

This book is a work of fiction. Names, characters, places, businesses, events, and incidents are either the products of the author's imagination or used in a fictitious manner. Any resemblance to actual persons, living or dead, or actual events is purely coincidental.

Cover design by [ COVER DESIGNER / "the author" ]
Editing by [ EDITOR / remove if none ]

ISBN: [ ISBN — or remove this line ]

[ EDITION, e.g. First Edition ] · [ MONTH YEAR ]

[ PUBLISHER / IMPRINT NAME ]
[ website or contact — optional ]

Line-by-line: The © line is the only legally required part — year of first publication plus the name that owns the rights (your real name or your pen name both work). All rights reserved is the standard rights statement. The fiction disclaimer protects you from "that character is me!" claims. ISBN is optional on Amazon (KDP gives you a free ASIN), but you need your own ISBN to go wide or sell print. Credit your cover designer and editor if your contract asks you to.

Variation A — KDP Select / Kindle Unlimited (Exclusive)

Amazon only

Use this if your ebook is enrolled in KDP Select and only sold on Amazon. Add this line to the standard page.

This ebook is licensed for your personal enjoyment only. This ebook may not be re-sold or given away to other people. Thank you for respecting the hard work of this author.

First published by [ PUBLISHER / IMPRINT ] in [ YEAR ].

Note: While in KDP Select you've promised Amazon exclusivity. Don't list other store links (Apple, Kobo, B&N) in your back matter during that term.

Variation B — Going Wide (Multiple Retailers)

Wide

Use this if you sell on Apple, Kobo, Barnes & Noble, Google Play, etc. You'll want your own ISBN.

Copyright © [ YEAR ] by [ AUTHOR / PEN NAME ]

Published worldwide by [ PUBLISHER / IMPRINT ].

Ebook ISBN: [ ISBN ]
Paperback ISBN: [ ISBN ]
Hardcover ISBN: [ ISBN — if applicable ]

Note: Each format (ebook, paperback, hardcover) needs its own ISBN. Buy them in your own name or imprint so you stay the publisher of record.

Variation C — Pen Name & Publishing Imprint

Pen name

Use this when you write under a pen name and/or publish under a business/imprint name. The copyright can be held by your legal name or your business — both are valid.

Written as [ PEN NAME ]

Copyright © [ YEAR ] [ LEGAL NAME or LLC / BUSINESS NAME ]

[ IMPRINT NAME ], an imprint of [ PUBLISHING COMPANY / LLC ]

Note: You don't have to reveal your legal name. Holding copyright in an LLC or business name keeps your pen name private while still protecting your rights. Just stay consistent across your books.

Variation D — AI Tools Disclosure (Optional)

Your call

Some authors add a line about AI use. This is optional and a personal/business decision — not legally required for books you authored. Pick the wording that's true for how you work, or leave it off entirely.

Option 1 — Human-authored, AI-assisted:
This book was written by a human author. AI tools were used to support the writing process (such as brainstorming, editing, and revision). The story, characters, and creative choices are the author's own.

Option 2 — No AI:
No generative AI was used in the creation of this book's text or cover.

Note: Retailer policies on AI disclosure change, and some platforms ask about AI use during upload rather than on the page itself. Decide what reflects your process and your brand. There's no single "right" answer here — choose what you can stand behind.
